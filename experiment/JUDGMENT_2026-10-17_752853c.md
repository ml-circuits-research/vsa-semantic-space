## 1. The reference bundle — THREE codes; the position code stays a declared option

Reason, from 14-bis: the unseen-transition gain is a prior over absolute positions — it helps a new formulation that places a role where the
training templates place it (T11 +0.37) and hurts one that does not (T4 subject −0.71, T7 recipient −0.44). That is the dependence on
position the whole design avoids: paraphrases change position, and a code that bets on position wins and loses by template rather than by
reading the route. The depth-3 gain (0.977 → 0.998) is real on these draws and comes from trained positions; it is a gain on an axis
already near one, and it is the same prior. Add a third more terms per sentence. So: the reference stays path + chainL + bigram
(10b-quinquies); B7 is recorded as "KEPT as a measured variant, outside the reference; reason: a surface-position prior (14-bis)"; the
omission rule (`pos_max`) is its stated behaviour. Legitimate use, declared per world: a legend may ask for the position code where the
language or the format has rigid order (forms, logs, code-like text); `sop train` exposes the code list for that reason only, and the
reference world's model does not carry it. N6 is released: on 10b-quinquies, as planned, with its criterion as written in the STATUS row.

## 2. The tool's presence calibration — adopt the FITTED threshold as the calibration RULE

The midpoint was never a principled choice; it was the default. The fit maximises balanced accuracy on the calibration rows and is reported
on the other half — that is rule 25 applied properly, not tuning. Adopt it as the tool's calibration rule for every role and every world
(not a recipient-specific patch): threshold per role = argmax of balanced accuracy on calibration rows that include held-out formulations
(for P1: a held-out share of the real sentences by hash), stated in config.json; the pooled midpoint printed beside it for one more round
as the comparison, then dropped. The recorded limit of the reader stands as written (≈10% overlap; 0.9 / 0.9 in 2 of 5 seeds).
The trade (about 9% of recipient-less sentences read as having one) is handled where the product already handles doubt, not by moving the
threshold: the distance of a sentence's presence ratio from the threshold is a presence MARGIN; it goes on the wire with the role's
`_margin` (the minimum of the two), so a near-threshold "present" is `_status` uncertain rather than a confident claim. One re-scoring from
the stored ratios, before adopting: of the false "present" and the missed "present" under the fitted threshold, what share lies within the
abstention band of the role's margin? Expectation written here: most. If so, the 9% are mostly "uncertain", which is the honest reading
of an overlap; if not, the asymmetry is declared per world (a legend may prefer misses to false alarms for an optional role) — a legend
field, not a hidden constant.
