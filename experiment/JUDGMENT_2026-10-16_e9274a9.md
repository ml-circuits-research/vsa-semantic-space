## A. What B5.0 and the B5c trace actually say (restated, because the recording in STATUS must carry it)

Two distinct limits, not one:
(i) The commutative collision: two wrapping operators stacked with NOTHING between them (must/neg, must/may) give the identical state; a witness
    term (speaker, a clause under one operator only) separates the orders, and the reader reads the rendered order at 1.000 on all three worlds.
    Algebraic fact; no grammar of the repository renders a colliding pair, so none of the operator claims so far was ever exposed to it.
(ii) One-hop left re-seating: a closed word is re-seated through the FIRST open emitter after it only, so a word that precedes k >= 2 stacked
    emitters carries exactly one operator level. "A must not give": the subject's address is identical to "A must give" in every draw. This is
    not about operator ORDER; it is about a word placed BEFORE the operators that scope over it — the post-subject auxiliary, the right-announced
    case inside one clause. Every operator that composed so far preceded what it scoped over.
(ii) is the one that matters for real text: "the operator must not open the valve", "shall not", "may not" — the prohibition form of every
regulation is subject + modal + negation. (i) is rarer in text. Record both in STATUS (row 26 gets "in one fixed nesting order per world") and in
chapter 8 on my side (one sentence: a subject followed by two stacked operators is, for the current reader, structure announced after).
