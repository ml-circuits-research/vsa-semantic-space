## A. Answers

Q1 (build the permuted-filler relational term now?). Not yet — there is a cheaper and more principled test first, and the design proposed in
Q1 has a causality problem that must be stated before anyone builds it.
  The causality problem: in "each A ... every B" the wide marker ('each') is read BEFORE the narrow noun (B). A term "emitted by the marker's
  category, holding the OTHER quantified noun under rho" needs B's identity at the moment 'each' is read; it is not available. The term would
  have to be emitted at the second determiner/noun, relative to a beacon the marker left — which makes the emission conditional on the marker
  having occurred, i.e. the relation again. Say this in RESULTS if the design is ever built; as written it cannot be traced (rule 11 would fail).
  C2c — a WORLD correction that removes the relation from the reader. Today the target makes 'every' emit the scope term in a one-quantifier
  sentence and NOT next to 'each': a conditional. Redefine the target so that every quantified determiner emits UNCONDITIONALLY, by category:
  marked determiners ('each', 'certain') draw their noun a second time at R_wide (x) slot; unmarked quantifiers ('every', 'some') at R_q (x) slot;
  'a' / 'the' nothing. Both are category-local, degree-1 terms, exactly what C2b showed composes. Which phrase is wide is then computed at
  READOUT by the legend, not by the reader: an R_wide term present -> that slot is wide and the R_q slot narrow; one R_q term alone -> the only
  quantifier; two R_q terms and no R_wide -> ambiguous, "both" (the unmarked case, which is what language really does). This is the same move as
  E2c and C2b (make the target producible by category-local terms) and it keeps the book's division of labour: the reader draws, the legend
  reasons. Rule 16: say which earlier sentences change (the two-quantifier rows become "two-quantifier, derived at readout"). Rule 11: trace
  one two-quantifier sentence at depth 2 and show the two second drawings at distinct addresses that both carry the 'that' prefix.
  Criterion: two-quantifier scope (derived) > 0.9 at depth 0 AND depth 2; one-quantifier unchanged (>= 0.99 slot); nouns >= 0.99; strict presence
  reported; ambiguous unmarked sentences -> "both" rate reported with its false-both rate; no loss on 6c / 9b / 9d / 10d. Decision rule: met ->
  quantifiers join the regularity entirely, H-b is retired as "a relation the legend computes from category-local terms", exception removed;
  not met -> the permuted-filler term is the next and last test, designed at the second noun relative to the marker's beacon, declared as a
  change of principle, with the same criterion. Run C2c on the slot target only (Q2).

Q3 (N5 design in the soft model). Define it without a new mechanism, by readout:
  (b) the ambiguous word gets a SUPERPOSED category vector, c = normalize(c_k1 + c_k2), and nothing else changes — every key that depends on the
  category (right emitter, left re-seating, relative terms) is then automatically the half-way key, so the word is drawn half-way in all codes
  with no special case. Resolution happens at READOUT by fit, as in N6/N7 and in "both hypotheses": the scanner tests the two kind hypotheses
  (the word's star under kind 1 at address a1, under kind 2 at a2) against the chart and reports the one with the larger cosine, with the
  margin between them. "Resolved" = margin > tau (stated); otherwise both are reported. The proposed "learned preference of the first open
  emitter after it" is a DIAGNOSTIC worth printing (does the gradient build such a preference?), not the definition; a definition must not
  depend on a quantity the training may or may not produce. Criterion as in the addendum (FULL within 0.02 of the unambiguous reference;
  resolved share and both-share reported; variant (a) as the baseline it is). Confirmed in that form; build.

Q4 (E1: "on all forms but the passives", or move the passives to the declared-limit row?). Neither yet. The E1 trace points to a cause that
has not been tested, and I disagree with the right-announced reading:
  - The 7c reference world (no predicate role) learned OPEN gates for 'that', 'to', 'by' (1.00 / 1.00 / 0.91) and had passives at 1.000 at depth
    3. The 10b world learned 'to' and 'by' CLOSED (open: '.', 'that', the recipient verbs). The trace shows the collision happens exactly because
    'to' and 'by' are closed: both persons hang off the period through P's two keys there, and the chain cannot tell T0 from T2. With 'to' and
    'by' open, C hangs off 'to' and A off 'by' — distinct addresses in both passives, and by the same mechanism as in 7c. The regression is
    therefore most likely the GATE SET the gradient chose once the predicate term and the recipient-less forms were present (E4 (b) showed 'to'
    rising to 0.40 without the recipient-less forms — it was pulled shut by them), not the passive form itself and not the predicate crosstalk.
  - E1b (one flag, 5 seeds, same seeds): relation-marker gates GIVEN open ('to', 'by', 'from'; rule 20 stated — the set 7c learned), participles
    and verbs learned. Rule 11 first: the T2 / T3 trace with this gate set, showing A and C at distinct addresses that both carry the 'that'
    prefix. Criterion: T2 / T3 C > 0.9 at depth 3, depth 3 FULL > 0.9, no loss on seen axes, unseen transitions reported (E1 (ii) cost 0.07
    there; this one should not, since 7c had these gates open at 0.74-0.79 unseen transitions). Decision rule: met -> the reference table is
    re-run once with the given relation-marker gates, the depth-3 claim is restated without an exception, and the gate choice is recorded as
    given (as 'that' is); not met -> "on all forms but the passives" becomes the final wording as a stated property of the predicate-role world.
  - Passives are not right-announced structure: 'to' and 'by' announce their fillers BEFORE them, and the passive is announced by "is given" before
